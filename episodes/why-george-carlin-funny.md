## Act 1 — The engine (0:45-3:00)
**Thesis: the prophet-curmudgeon.** Carlin treats any soft phrase as a
crime scene and reconstructs what actually happened from the words people
chose not to use. Rigidify + literalize at max: hold the euphemism to
its own literal meaning until it breaks in public.

The mechanism, beat by beat:
1. **The list.** The sermon form: accelerate through examples (rising
   rate, rising pitch) — the room is carried forward by the rhythm before
   they know where it's going.
2. **The key word.** Hard-stop on the euphemism being executed ("passed
   away," "persons of substance"). The stop does the indignant work.
3. **The restart.** The list engine starts again. Rhetorical timing, not
   confessional — closer to preaching than to joking, which is why it
   outlives jokes.

Theory links: rigidity (the rule applied until it confesses) +
literalize (soft language held to hard meaning) + status_reversal (the
little guy prosecuting the language of power) + bisociation.

## Act 3 — The three bits that prove it (5:00-8:00)
1. **The seven dirty words** — a language taboo argued as a constitutional
   question. The bit that reached the Supreme Court (FCC v. Pacifica,
   1978). Rigidify as civic action.
2. **Euphemism execution** — "passed away," "persons of substance," "shell
   shock" → "post-traumatic stress disorder." Each softening gets
   dissected until the motive shows.
3. **The list as sermon** — the form itself: acceleration, key word, stop,
   restart. Imitated by every rant comic since; matched by none at scale.

## Closer (8:00-9:00)
Carlin is the control group of this entire project: if your methodology
can't put him at or near the top, your methodology is broken. Ours puts
him #1 (consensus, canon), #2 (era), #3 (house), #6 (people's) — and #1
in measured joke quality. Five different elections, one winner. That's
not a crown, that's calibration. Verdict: the standard everything else
is measured against.
