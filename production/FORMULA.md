## THE 11-BEAT ARC

Every gold video, regardless of length or subject, runs this order:

```
BEAT  1  ENGINE NAMED          the format's thesis in one line
BEAT  2  POETIC INTRO          epithet pile → personal claim
BEAT  3  SPECIFIC THESIS       "how X started and evolved"
BEAT  4  CROSS-DOMAIN LENS     define the device via film/music FIRST
BEAT  5  BRIDGE BACK           self-aware channel voice
BEAT  6  PERSONA LAYER         character-creation suite before the machine
BEAT  7  CASE STUDY            one special, chronological, clip IS text
BEAT  8  SECOND SPECIMEN       same device, different comedian
BEAT  9  ACCIDENTAL GENESIS    how they discovered their own machine
BEAT 10  HUMAN UNIVERSAL       mechanism → claim about being alive
BEAT 11  SUBJECT NAMES ENGINE  end with THEM explaining their theory
```

### Beat-by-beat with gold examples

**1. ENGINE NAMED (first 30 seconds)**
Name the FORMAT's engine, not the comedian's bio. One sentence.
- Carlin: "hello Cindy I am the architect" → rhythm never wavered
- Acaster: "stand-up is a brilliant trojan horse"
- Locke: "he will always surprise you and always do the sixth option out of five"
- Louis (Nerdwriter): "207 words, chosen very purposefully"

**2. POETIC INTRO (0:40–1:50)**
Absurd epithet list → personal claim. The list IS the character study.
- Acaster: "scrape master, cabbage blaster, pudding lover…" → "one of my favorites"
- Carlin: career evolutions list (people pleaser → spoken word hippie → raging renegade)

**3. SPECIFIC THESIS (1:10–2:00)**
"How he does this" and "how he pulls it off so effectively" — two questions, one video.
- Acaster: "how he plays with a technique known as the unreliable narrator"
- Carlin: "how kin's use of Rhythm started and evolved"

**4. CROSS-DOMAIN LENS (2:00–5:30)**
Define the device in FILM, MUSIC, or NOVEL terms before touching comedy. Fully retell one non-comedy example.
- Acaster: Shutter Island fully retold (2 min) — unreliable narrator via film
- Carlin: Danny Kaye, radio DJ, xylophone — rhythm via music history
- Locke: McIntyre "follows a great recipe" vs Lock "combining weird flavors" — cooking analogy
- Effect: viewer learns a PORTABLE FRAME, then applies it to comedy. Makes the return to comedy feel like discovery.

**5. BRIDGE BACK (5:19–5:33)**
Self-aware channel voice. The narrator talks to the audience about the video itself.
- Acaster: "Okay. Excellent. BUT! How does this fit into the world of stand-up? Isn't this channel about stand-up? Aren't you gonna start talking about stand-up? Isn't this video about James Acaster you giant plum?!"
- Rule: the narrator is a CHARACTER (fan with a notebook), not a professor.

**6. PERSONA LAYER (6:18–12:12)**
Dedicated act for the stage persona BEFORE the machine. Structure:
  a. Video-game character-creation analogy (Burr heightens anger, Moran puts logic into whimsy)
  b. The subject's persona in their OWN WORDS (interview clips)
  c. The real person reveal (the shock of seeing them without it)
  d. History of persona development (years of tinkering, failed attempts)
- Acaster: Stuart Goldsmith interview → "unjustifiably confident idiot" → Ross Noble imitation → years of tinkering
- Locke: Jack D + Mighty Boa → "grounded person and absurd person" → protected vs unprotected self
- Carlin: hippie tenor → Patrick's voice advice → vitriolic philosopher

**7. CASE STUDY (12:00–25:00)**
One special/bit, walked chronologically. The clip IS the text.
- Acaster: Recognise — banana revenge → Springleaf reveal → wife leaving → seaside photo dilemma
- Locke: the cat-stretching bit, the swan toilets, the recycling routine
- Carlin: Seven Words 1977 vs 1978 side-by-side
- Overlays: operator labels, pause timers, BV scores, rhythm markers

**8. SECOND SPECIMEN (23:38–29:19)**
Same device, DIFFERENT comedian. Proves the device is a device, not one person's quirk.
- Acaster video: Lee's Carpet Remnant World — the failed callback opens 20 min of revelation
- Rule: the second comic must do the device DIFFERENTLY, not just similarly.

**9. ACCIDENTAL GENESIS (29:28–34:19)**
How they discovered their own machine. Usually: creative low point → stopped doing X → accidental discovery → friend's advice → meta-reveal.
- Acaster: hated writing → stopped writing → undercover cop bit → breakup bled through → Nish Kumar: "you owe it to the audience" → "undercover cop pretending to be stand-up comic"
- Locke: "it was an accident" → "I just wanted to talk about being an undercover cop" → "I was going through all this stuff of not knowing who I was"
- Carlin: "one night by accident it will happen the correct way and then you save it"

**10. HUMAN UNIVERSAL (40:18–44:34)**
Zoom out from comedy to a claim about being alive.
- Acaster: "We are all unreliable narrators, to some degree, in our own lives"
- Locke: "there's a childishness and there's an innocence to it… trying to make them laugh in the same way you would try to make your friends laugh on the school ground"
- Carlin: "don't forget what we do is oratory it's a rhetoric it's not just comedy"

**11. SUBJECT NAMES ENGINE (final 2 min)**
End with the COMEDIAN explaining their own theory. Not the narrator.
- Carlin: jester → philosopher → poet triptych (Arthur Koestler's Act of Creation)
- Acaster: "No comics ever done a whole career in this universe… I can do that forever"
- Locke: Robin Williams quote → "Shawn kept it… he only had to be the funniest"
- Louis (Nerdwriter): "like great poetry, great stand-up comedy is language distilled"

## TONE RULES

These are non-negotiable. Break one and it stops feeling like gold.

1. **Personal obsession as frame.** "one of my favorites", "I was completely obsessed", "I've watched it a hundred times". The narrator IS a fan who studied the material. Not a critic. Not a professor. A fan with a notebook.

2. **Interview-as-evidence.** The interview clips ARE the analysis, not decoration. When the subject explains their own mechanism, that's the most authoritative evidence possible.

3. **Theory naming without jargon.** "Reality bridging" (Adam Bloom), "protected vs unprotected self", "practical effect theory", "sixth option". NAME the mechanisms but use accessible language. No academic vocabulary in the narration. (Our operator names live in the OVERLAYS, not the voiceover.)

4. **The clip IS the text.** Never narrate over a punchline. Play clip → pause → analyze → resume. The clip and the analysis are the same object.

5. **Deeper reading arrives organically.** "On a deeper level, Louis is actually trying to make a comment on the ruthlessness of the economy." The theory emerges FROM the material, not on top of it.

6. **Honest uncertainty.** "I've struggled with this as a research assignment… Sean is so hard to make sense of." Admit when analysis is hard. That's credibility, not weakness.

7. **Chapter titles ARE thesis statements.** "The sixth option superpower." "Grounding the absurd." "The protected vs unprotected self." Not "Section 5: Analysis."

8. **Numbers as credibility hooks.** "207 words, not a single one wasted." "25 words in 14 seconds." "18 individual phenomena in 46 words in 20 seconds." "nearly a second faster." Specific counts say: I counted. I'm serious.

9. **Self-aware channel voice.** "Isn't this video about James Acaster you giant plum?!" "I've played it already but that moment…" The narrator is a character who knows they're making a video.

10. **Critique before reverence.** The fairness bracket: "others like the hair poem feel dated", "standup has outgrown this style of delivery", "there is also by his own admission Indulgence". Earn the praise by being critical first.

11. **Clip fragments 5–15 seconds.** Short enough to analyze, long enough to feel the mechanism.

12. **The narrator NEVER talks over a punchline.** Pause the clip, analyze, resume. Respect the material.

## THE FORMAT FORMULA (synthesis)

```
OPEN:       Engine named + poetic intro + specific thesis
LENS:       Cross-domain definition (film/music/novel) + bridge back
PERSONA:    Character-creation suite + real-person reveal + history
CASE:       One special, chronological, clip IS text, overlays fire
SPECIMEN:   Second comic does the device differently
GENESIS:    Accidental discovery → friend's advice → meta-reveal
UNIVERSAL:  Mechanism → claim about being alive
CLOSE:      Subject names their own engine + bookend clip + next hook
```
